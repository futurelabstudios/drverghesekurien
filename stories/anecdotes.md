## Told by IRMA or dairy alumni

The people in this group passed through the Institute of Rural Management Anand as students, or identify themselves by an IRMA batch and then worked in the dairy system. Tushaar Shah, who directed IRMA and tells several of the sharpest stories, was an NDDB economist rather than an IRMA student. His accounts are with colleagues, below.

### Kings do not live in pigsties

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, who sat the IRMA entrance in early 1982 and later taught at the institute for about six years. He published the essay on 17 April 2006.

**When and where.** His first days in Anand, in what students called the Farmers’ Hostel on the NDDB campus.

**The story.** Students had been told to expect modest rooms. They found the hostel well fitted out. Kurien, Sriram writes, told them that kings do not live in pigsties, that they were his princes, and that he had built the institute to send a thousand Kuriens into the field. One such person, he said, would be enough to imagine the change.

Sriram also records the practical bait in the advertisement of that time: a stipend of Rs 600 a month as a student, and a job at the end at not less than Rs 1,200.

**Source.** [M. S. Sriram, “Kurien: A Personal Essay”](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### What the milk queue does to caste

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, same essay. He says he heard Kurien say this, and that it moved him to tears. Years later he decided the eloquence had not, by itself, remade village society. He includes that second thought. It belongs with the memory.

**The story.** Kurien would thunder about people standing in line to pour milk. What does it do to a high-caste Brahmin, he asked, to stand behind a Harijan who arrived earlier? Is that only orderly collection, or is it also a blow to the caste system?

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### Village Kurien

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, who is careful to say he was told the story and did not see it. He does not name the person who told him. It is here because the teller is named and the episode is specific, and the gap is marked.

**The story.** As Sriram heard it, Professor C. T. Kurien introduced himself — “I am C. T. Kurien” — and Verghese Kurien answered that he was V. Kurien, “Village Kurien,” playing on “city.”

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### The headlamps had wipers

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, from his student years.

**The story.** One campus pastime was to look at the chairman’s car, which was always among the best in town. In those years it was a Peugeot. What stuck with the students was that the headlamps had wipers.

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### The room freshener

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, recalling a visit by Kurien to address the IRMA faculty, in the years when Sriram was on the staff.

**The story.** Someone had sprayed the room with a jasmine freshener. Kurien walked in, sniffed, and told the faculty, straight-faced, that the place smelt like a brothel. He paused, Sriram writes, and added that they would not know, and should ask him. Sriram’s reading is that the line was for effect, which was how Kurien played a room.

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### A speech in English, and again in Gujarati

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, about his own convocation. This is a later ceremony than the first convocation, which Tushaar Shah describes with Indira Gandhi. Sriram names the speaker as Vice President Hidayatullah. Sriram’s batch entered around 1982, so he is describing one of the early convocations. He does not give the day.

**When and where.** An IRMA convocation, with thousands of farmers present because Kurien had invited them.

**The story.** Hidayatullah spoke partly in English and partly in Hindi. Kurien delivered his own speech in English and had it translated into Gujarati and read out as well. Sriram offers this as Kurien in full voice, unwilling to leave the farmers in the crowd outside the language of the ceremony.

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### The seminar, and then the press

**Who told it.** M. S. Sriram, writing in 2006 about a seminar at IRMA on the joint ventures NDDB wanted with state cooperatives. Kumar Anshul, of the IRMA batch of 2001–03, wrote in the comments on the same page that he was a second-year student at the time and felt the same let-down. Robin Mathew (PRM 23) and others argued in those comments that Kurien was defending cooperative autonomy, not joint ventures as such. The dispute is part of the record of the story.

**The story.** Sriram received a letter, signed by Kurien, inviting him to the seminar. For two days people argued. The NDDB side, as he recalls it, said they still wanted Kurien’s guidance. At the end Kurien spoke to the press. Sriram writes that the press account was presented as the seminar’s consensus, and that it did not match what he had heard in the room. He felt used. He says he was in tears because the hero looked unwilling to see that he was losing the argument.

He also describes, as his own account of the 2006 exit from the Gujarat federation, a package of perks — a car for Kurien and for his wife, a cook — and the transfer of his assistant, Joseph, to Kolkata. Sriram writes that Kurien pleaded to keep Joseph as part of the package. Readers of the essay, including people who had worked in the dairy system, answered that this ending was unfair to a man whose institutions were being taken from him in a political fight. *Frontline*’s news report of the resignation is in [Debates](../debates.md). It does not mention Joseph. Treat the assistant’s transfer as Sriram’s account.

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html), including the comments by Kumar Anshul and Robin Mathew.

### You forgot, or you did not want me there

**Who told it.** Rajat Singhal, PRM 24 (2003–05), in a comment on Sriram’s essay.

**When and where.** Kurien’s chamber at IRMA, when Singhal went to invite him to Jatra 2005. Singhal writes that Kurien had not been in the habit of attending the opening, which is why the invitation was late.

**The story.** Singhal admitted the lapse. Kurien said, “You forgot, or you did not want to invite me,” and smiled. Singhal writes that the smile settled the point, and that he had no answer.

**Source.** [Comment on Sriram’s essay](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### Be true Irmans

**Who told it.** Arpit Shah, PRM 30 (2009–11), in a comment on the same essay.

**When and where.** A meeting with Kurien on 8 April, during Shah’s course. He does not write the year. Kurien was ill and away from Anand for part of that batch’s induction, so the meeting came later. A Wikimedia photograph of Kurien with students is dated 8 April 2009. This archive does not claim it is a picture of Shah’s meeting. See [CREDITS](../media/photos/CREDITS.md).

**The story.** The class had wanted to meet him in the first week and could not. When he came in, Shah writes, the room went quiet. For the first several minutes Kurien kept returning to one point: IRMA was the place where influence could not buy admission, and if anyone knew of a case they should tell him and he would act. Then the wit returned, with help from his wife, because his memory was failing. Asked what he expected of them, he answered, “Be true Irmans.” He signed copies of *I Too Had a Dream*. Shah noticed the hands were swollen, and that Kurien signed anyway.

**Source.** [Comment by Arpit Shah](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### I could feed the country because I am stubborn

**Who told it.** Arpit Shah, reporting what Shyamlal Gupta, whom Shah also calls an IRMA alumnus, told him. Gupta had met Kurien in a Mumbai hospital some months before Shah wrote. This is secondhand. A different hospital story, from Ajay Gupta, is below. They do not match, and both are kept.

**The story.** As Shah retells it, a nurse had been trying to get Kurien to take milk. She called him stubborn. He answered that he had been able to feed the country milk because he was stubborn.

**Source.** [Comment by Arpit Shah](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html)

### Do not do that on this campus

**Who told it.** Ajay Gupta, PRM 6 (1985–87).

**When and where.** Within a week of joining IRMA. Two students were walking from Jagnath toward the campus to fetch an evening snack.

**The story.** A car came up behind them. Out of habit they stuck out a thumb. The car stopped. Kurien was inside. He lowered the glass, told them not to repeat that on this campus, and drove on.

**Source.** [Ajay Gupta, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/four-occasions-four-memories-and-four-messages/)

### Place a dynamite in Krishi Bhawan

**Who told it.** Ajay Gupta, by then an alumnus, working on deputation from IPCL to the Ministry of Agriculture.

**When and where.** Around September 1993, outside Krishi Bhawan, New Delhi.

**The story.** Gupta’s small team had a sanctioned budget of Rs 250 crore to promote plastics in agriculture, and then ran into official obstruction. He saw Kurien step out of a car, introduced himself as an IRMA person, and asked for advice. Kurien listened to where they were stuck. His answer, as Gupta records it: if you really want to contribute to this nation, place a dynamite in Krishi Bhawan.

**Source.** [Ajay Gupta, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/four-occasions-four-memories-and-four-messages/)

### No one is greater than the other

**Who told it.** Ajay Gupta.

**When and where.** About June 2012, at Kurien’s house in Anand. A senior professor who was in touch with Kurien arranged a meeting of about fifteen minutes. Kurien was unwell.

**The story.** Gupta bent to touch his feet. Kurien stepped back and told him not to do that with anyone, because no one is greater than the other. Molly joined them and did most of the talking. She made the tea and served it. The house, Gupta writes, had little furniture, and only what was useful. They asked about his work and his family.

**Source.** [Ajay Gupta, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/four-occasions-four-memories-and-four-messages/)

### The nurse said she would do to him what he did to the country

**Who told it.** Ajay Gupta, reporting a nurse at a Mumbai hospital. He had gone there after the Anand visit and was not allowed in. The nurse’s milk story is what the nurse told Gupta, not a scene he saw.

**The story.** Kurien was refusing food and drink. The nurse told Gupta that she had said to Kurien that he had made the whole country drink milk, and that she would do the same to him. Gupta writes that she succeeded. Kurien’s daughter told Gupta that GCMMF was supporting the family and that they would manage.

Gupta also writes that the nurse said Kurien’s blood was hard to obtain. That is a medical claim from one conversation. It is not treated here as a clinical fact.

**Source.** [Ajay Gupta, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/four-occasions-four-memories-and-four-messages/)

### I have given you the best

**Who told it.** Madhusudan Manvi, PRM 4. He writes that classmates, not necessarily he himself, went to wish Kurien on his birthday and asked why the IRMA hostel was so well appointed.

**The story.** Kurien’s answer, as Manvi records it: “I have given you the best. I now expect the best from you.”

**Source.** [Madhusudan Manvi, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/give-the-best-to-expect-best/)

### The key went into his pocket

**Who told it.** Dhiraj Hazarika, IRMA 1987, then working for GCMMF and posted in Guwahati.

**When and where.** Early 1990s, NDDB’s main building in Anand. The team was led by B. M. Vyas, then managing director of GCMMF. They had come to present a test launch and a marketing plan for a new product in the North-East.

**The story.** They arrived early. On the porch stood an Ambassador without a driver. Parking there was forbidden. As the meeting time neared there was a noise outside. Kurien had seen the car, taken the key, and gone up to his office. The key was later returned, and the car was moved. Hazarika tells it as a lesson delivered without a speech.

**Source.** [Dhiraj Hazarika, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/rules-must-be-followed-to-maintain-discipline/)

## Told by colleagues and farmers

### One way to skin a cat

**Who told it.** Tushaar Shah, who joined NDDB in 1977 as a young economist, later worked in Kurien’s office, and became director of IRMA. He is emeritus professor at IRMA. He published this remembrance on 17 September 2026.

**When and where.** The last day of Shah’s induction at NDDB. Kurien was 55, chairman of NDDB and of Amul. Twelve new officers sat in front of him.

**The story.** Shah had been ready to leave. In two weeks he had seen a senior officer dressed down in public by the chief executive, and five young officers made to do sit-ups for coming late. He does not say Kurien personally ordered the sit-ups. He says the place looked like a theory of management that assumes people must be controlled.

Then Kurien talked for forty-five minutes. Nobody interrupted, and nobody wanted to. He went from what he called his garage days, and from Tribhuvandas Patel persuading him to make Anand home, to the case against government milk schemes and multinational companies. He said NDDB had built a first set of Amul-pattern unions — sixteen in one version of Shah’s text, eighteen in the copy posted on Vrikshamandir — and that India needed about two hundred more. Shah quotes him: people will say there are many ways to skin a cat; at NDDB there was one, the Amul pattern. Shah stayed. Anand became his home.

**Sources.** [Tushaar Shah on Vrikshamandir, 17 September 2026](https://vrikshamandir.com/2026/09/17/remembering-kurien-the-man-who-changed-countless-lives/); [the same text on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tushaar-shah-02277278_verghesekurien-amul-cooperatives-activity-7506054247218761728-aPJZ)

### Dalaya did the work

**Who told it.** Tushaar Shah, from the years he worked in Kurien’s office during Operation Flood II planning.

**The story.** Kurien would call him in to tell stories about encounters with the powerful — Shah names Nehru, Alexei Kosygin, Morarji Desai, and Shastri. Shah does not retell those encounters. What he does quote is Kurien’s division of labour: in the Amul days, Dalaya did the work, and Kurien did the talking.

The episodes about those four leaders, as Kurien told them to Shah, are listed in [UNVERIFIED.md](../UNVERIFIED.md) because the telling is attested and the tales themselves were not published.

**Source.** [Tushaar Shah, Vrikshamandir](https://vrikshamandir.com/2026/09/17/remembering-kurien-the-man-who-changed-countless-lives/)

### May their tribe perish

**Who told it.** Tushaar Shah. The line was written by Shah at Kurien’s instruction, for Kurien to speak. It is Kurien’s joke in the sense that he ordered it and delivered it. It is Shah’s sentence on the page.

**The story.** Kurien disliked economists. For a presidential address to their annual conference he had Shah write that he hated them because they are never where the action is, and arrive later only to find fault, and that their tribe should perish. Shah writes that the address got a standing ovation.

**Source.** [Tushaar Shah, Vrikshamandir](https://vrikshamandir.com/2026/09/17/remembering-kurien-the-man-who-changed-countless-lives/)

### We pour milk

**Who told it.** P. S. Mohan, who joined Kurien’s secretariat at IRMA in November 1998, after Kurien stepped down from NDDB and moved his office. Mohan wrote “Up Close and Personal” for IRMA’s *Network* magazine, in the December 2012 issue in memory of Kurien. The first days he dealt with P. A. Joseph, Kurien’s executive assistant. When Joseph went on leave, Mohan went in alone. He compares it to a mouse entering a lion’s den.

**When and where.** A summer day at IRMA, with a group of visitors, in the years of guest programmes that also took people to NDDB and Amul.

**The story.** A visitor asked how the lawns stayed green. Kurien said they did not water the lawns. He paused, threw up both hands, and said they poured milk — “our heart.” The group laughed.

Mohan’s wider portrait, from the same months: Kurien wanted the job done at once, and a straight answer. If you did not know, you said so. He arrived before the time fixed. Letters did not sit. Mohan says Kurien described his standard as “integrity, integrity and integrity,” and gives Molly Kurien the credit of having stood behind him.

**Source.** [P. S. Mohan, IRMA *Network*, December 2012](https://www.irma.ac.in/faculty-research/research-and-publications/network/current-and-past-issues?issueid=180&tab=2)

### He crossed out the name

**Who told it.** P. S. Mohan, from earlier, when he worked in the IRMA director’s office and Kurien was chairman.

**The story.** Draft board agendas went to the chairman. A member from the rural development ministry had been transferred and was no longer on the board. Mohan forgot to drop the name. Kurien crossed it out. Mohan told Tushaar Shah, then director. Shah laughed and said that was why Kurien was chairman.

**Source.** [P. S. Mohan, IRMA *Network*](https://www.irma.ac.in/faculty-research/research-and-publications/network/current-and-past-issues?issueid=180&tab=2)

### Pulled out of Salem

The meeting with M. G. Ramachandran is under [political visits](#sell-a-refrigerator-to-an-eskimo). What follows is a later episode from the same officer.

**Who told it.** Shantha Sheela Nair, later a secretary to the Government of India, who had been special officer for the oilseeds mission in Tamil Nadu.

**When and where.** After M. G. Ramachandran’s death. Nair does not date it. She had become Collector of Tiruchirappalli and had got the district into Operation Flood. A new state government sent her to Salem. Kurien did not call her. He asked Parthasarathy, joint secretary for dairy in Delhi and a Tamil Nadu-cadre officer, whether she would come to Delhi. She said yes. Kurien then told Cabinet Secretary T. N. Seshan, whom Nair had worked with when Seshan was agriculture secretary in Tamil Nadu, that he wanted her in the Dairy Technology Mission. Seshan got her out of Salem.

**The story, in her words for the moral.** She looked like a war hero. Her general, she writes, was Kurien.

**Source.** [Shantha Sheela Nair, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/memories-of-dr-v-kurien/)

### Aya Ram, Gaya Ram, and now Jai Ram

**Who told it.** Shantha Sheela Nair, from her time in the Technology Mission in Delhi.

**When and where.** A rough meeting with Sam Pitroda and the agriculture secretary. Jairam Ramesh was laying out how the missions should be run. Kurien waited.

**The story.** When Ramesh finished, Kurien opened with a pun on the old phrase for political defectors: he had seen Aya Rams and Gaya Rams, and now he had to deal with a Jai Ram. Nair writes that Pitroda and the room laughed, and that the rest of the meeting went Kurien’s way.

**Source.** [Shantha Sheela Nair, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/memories-of-dr-v-kurien/)

### The bulldozer, the cigarette tin, and a job he said he did not have

**Who told it.** Amrita Patel, to Vinay Umarji of Rediff, published 12 September 2012. She came to Anand at 15, when her father retired and settled there. She later took a degree in veterinary science and succeeded Kurien at NDDB.

**The story.** Amul was the only place in town with a bulldozer. She wondered how it worked. One day Kurien put her in it with him and showed her. He smoked, she says, like a chimney, from a tin of Red & White.

After her degree she asked him for a job and felt sure of herself. He said there was no place: Amul was an all-male outfit. She offered to work unpaid, and was attached to three veterinary doctors. Seven or eight months later the general manager — Kurien — called. A cattle-feed factory was up. An FAO nutritionist was coming, she was a woman, and she wanted a woman assistant. The honorary worker had a job.

Patel told the interviewer that she used to ask him what would happen when the next cooperative did not have a Kurien. He used to answer that if you had a Tribhuvandas, you would have a Kurien. Her own view, looking back, was that finding the Kuriens would be just as hard. She describes the early team as Tribhuvandas, whose thinking stayed with the farmers, and two professionals — Kurien and H. M. Dalaya — whose integrity kept decisions on that side.

**Source.** [Rediff, 12 September 2012](http://www.rediff.com/money/report/interview-amrita-patel-on-kurien-ideologies/20120912.htm)

### Farmers in the middle of an interview

**Who told it.** Rasheeda Bhagat, who interviewed Kurien at his office in the Amul headquarters in Anand in 1998 and 1999, for *Business Line*. She published this account on 13 September 2012. The 1998 interview was soon after he had handed NDDB to Amrita Patel. He was 76.

**The story.** Mid-interview, a large group of farmers arrived from a distant cooperative. They would have to go back the same day. He asked Bhagat’s leave and let them in. They tried to touch his feet, put a coloured headgear on him, and gave him an ornamental sword. He joked with them for five or ten minutes and picked up the interview where he had left it.

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, *Business Line*, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### Where should we bury him

**Who told it.** R. S. Sodhi, managing director of GCMMF, recalling the night of 9 September 2012. The recollection was published in November 2018, when Sodhi was answering a different controversy. Nirmala Kurien’s account of the same wish is in [Life](../life.md).

**When and where.** Muljibhai Patel Urological Hospital, Nadiad, in the small hours after Kurien died.

**The story.** Sodhi and a colleague reached the hospital and asked Nirmala and Molly where he should be buried, because he had been born a Christian. They said his wish was to be cremated. He was cremated at the same place as Tribhuvandas Patel. Sodhi adds that when Molly died in Mumbai about three months later, she was cremated too.

**Source.** [Indian Express, 26 November 2018](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/ignore-such-remarks-verghese-kuriens-daughter-on-conversion-charge-5464927/)

## Told by Kurien in sourced interviews

These are stories he told a journalist or a convocation, as that listener published them. They are not taken from his books. One autobiography episode is included at the end, and only as a reviewer’s page-cited paraphrase, because this research did not read the book.

### That happiness was infectious

**Who told it.** Kurien, to the graduates of the National Institute of Design, Ahmedabad, at its 19th convocation. Rediff reported the speech on 8 December 1998, just after he had retired from NDDB. He was 77.

**The story.** He said he had wanted, like other young men of his time, a place among the captains of industry. Then he met Tribhuvandas Patel, who could have sought a greater standing and instead found his happiness serving the dairy farmers of Kheda. That happiness was infectious, and Kurien caught it. Fifty years on he had not left Anand, did not want to, and did not regret a moment.

He also gave the graduates ten pieces of advice, summarised in [Quotes](../quotes.md).

**Source.** [Rediff, 8 December 1998](https://www.rediff.com/business/1998/dec/08kurien.htm)

### What is pasteurisation?

**Who told it.** Kurien, to Rasheeda Bhagat, in the 1998 and 1999 interviews she published on 13 September 2012.

**The story.** He said the selection committee for the government scholarship was chaired by the Chief Justice of India, who asked him what pasteurisation was. He said it was something to do with milk. The justice said that was enough: he was selected, because no other engineer in the room knew, and India did not yet have a pasteurisation plant. Kurien asked for metallurgy instead. The answer was this scholarship, or nothing.

He then said he took his revenge by doing a master’s in metallurgy and nuclear physics, because an underground explosion had turned his ambition toward nuclear engineering. Amul, GCMMF, and the World Food Prize describe the Michigan State degree as mechanical engineering, with dairy engineering as a minor. The institutional record and this interview are both in [Life](../life.md). The interview is not used to overwrite the degree.

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, *Business Line*, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### The green hat and the yellow trousers

**Who told it.** Kurien, to Rasheeda Bhagat, same interviews.

**The story.** He said an under secretary disliked his green hat and yellow trousers and packed him off to “a dump called Anand,” near Bombay, as a Class I officer on Rs 350 a month, “to see cows.” He protested that TISCO had paid him twice that. The contract held. A different interview tradition, in the *Business Line* profile of 9 September 2012, gives the Anand salary as Rs 275. Both numbers are left standing.

In the same profile he is reported to have said that he understood Gujarati and refused to speak it, so that he would know what others were saying when they thought he did not.

**Sources.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece); [*The Hindu Business Line*, 9 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/opinion/The-man-who-empowered-through-milk/article20499872.ece)

### Cows. What else?

**Who told it.** Kurien, to Rasheeda Bhagat.

**The story.** When the King of Thailand wanted to meet him, the Indian ambassador said ministers asked for that appointment and never got it, and asked what was special about Kurien. Kurien’s answer: “Cows. What else?”

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### They travel only first class

**Who told it.** Kurien and Amrita Patel, separately, to Rasheeda Bhagat. The press was already writing, in December 1998, about a plan to send fifty buffaloes to the King of Thailand for the fiftieth year of his reign.

**The story.** Patel called it one of his three-in-the-morning ideas. He told her, she said, that he would give her a lot of silver jewellery to wear as a shepherdess, and that she could keep it, so she agreed. Shipping fell through. She rang the managing director of Air India to charter a plane. He said flying buffaloes would cost a fortune. The promise had been made.

Kurien, straight-faced, asked Bhagat whether she knew that the buffaloes travel only first class. Air India was worried about the smell. His plan, as he told it, was to feed them little, give them nothing to drink, and sedate them, because two buffaloes fighting could wreck the plane. On the cost, he shrugged toward the board’s profits. Bhagat writes that you could not always tell when the deadpan was a joke.

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### Not everybody is for sale

**Who told it.** Kurien, to Rasheeda Bhagat, about a multinational that tried to head off an NDDB joint venture in Sri Lanka.

**The story.** He would not go to Delhi to meet the company’s chief. The man came to him and asked what would become of them. Kurien said that was not his concern. It was time, he said, that the white man understood that not every Indian is for sale. A few cannot be bought, and they would defend India’s business.

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### The Pakistan remark

**Who told it.** Kurien, to Rasheeda Bhagat. She sets it among the stories he told with a face that left the listener unsure.

**The story.** Asked about helping Pakistan raise its milk yield, he said they had liberalised, globalised, and eaten their cows. He said he was a Christian and had no religious sentiment about the cow, and that he still would not agree to good cows being eaten. Eat the useless ones, he said, and if there were not enough he could be told, and some could be smuggled for the table. On exporting milk to Pakistan he said that if milk could go from Delhi to Calcutta, it could go from Delhi to Lahore, and that the practical step was to bribe one guard.

This archive repeats the remark because he gave it to a journalist and she published it, and because she warned that it sat inside a style of deadpan provocation. It is not a programme document.

**Source.** [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece)

### You continue to teach them how to suck on cigars

**Who told it.** Kurien, in his autobiography *I Too Had a Dream*. This research did not read the book. M. S. Sriram, in the 2006 essay, quotes the episode and gives a page (p. 212 in the edition he used). The paraphrase here follows that quotation and does not reproduce it.

**The story.** At a board meeting of the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad, members were discussing why graduates did not go into development work. One of them, cigar in hand, asked whether Kurien wanted their graduates to go and milk cows. Kurien stood up and told him to go on teaching them how to suck on cigars.

A harsher scene from the same book, a confrontation in a minister’s office, is named in [UNVERIFIED.md](../UNVERIFIED.md) only as a page citation. It is not retold here.

**Source.** [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html), citing *I Too Had a Dream*.

## Visits by political leaders

### Nehru, Indira, and the powder plant

**Who told it.** Harish Damodaran, *The Indian Express*, 2 March 2024, in a reported account of Ajarpura. He is not a witness to 1955. He is the journalist who places the visit.

**When and where.** 31 October 1955, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel’s birth anniversary, at Ajarpura, about nine years before Shastri’s night there. The occasion was the inauguration of Amul’s buffalo-milk powder plant, which Damodaran calls the world’s first.

**The story.** Jawaharlal Nehru came with his daughter Indira Gandhi. Damodaran’s point of contrast is with 1964: Nehru came to see and to inaugurate. Shastri wanted to stay, and to talk without a crowd of police.

**Source.** [*The Indian Express*, 2 March 2024](https://indianexpress.com/article/long-reads/union-code-0001-how-a-village-seeded-indias-white-revolution-9191324/)

### The foreigner who was the Prime Minister

**Who told it.** Harish Damodaran, same article. The overnight stay is also in a *Times of India* report on Ajarpura’s 75 years, which quotes R. S. Sodhi and Gayatri Patel, chairperson of the Ajarpura society, and in a speech by NDDB chairman Meenesh Shah on 20 December 2022.

**When and where.** The evening of 30 October 1964, at the house of Ramanbhai Punjabhai Patel in Ajarpura, about 15 kilometres from Anand. The next day, 31 October, Shastri inaugurated the 50-tonne cattle-feed plant at Kanjari, about 8 kilometres from the dairy. A couple of days before, Kurien had heard from Gujarat chief minister Balwantrai Mehta that the Prime Minister wanted a night in a farmer’s house, not only a ceremony.

**The story.** Kurien asked Ramanbhai to host a guest and did not say who was coming. Damodaran reports the line Kurien used: the guest was a foreigner he knew. Ramanbhai asked how such a man would stay, and what he would eat. Kurien told him foreigners are like that, and to keep the place tidy, with a clean bathroom, and to serve the food the family ate.

The visitor arrived around 6.30 in the evening. He talked to people about the cooperative, ate a simple dinner in Ramanbhai’s tobacco shed, and went to bed around 1 a.m. In the morning he watched milk being received at the society, then went to Kanjari, then to Anand to talk with Kurien at length.

On 2 December 1964 Shastri wrote to every chief minister. He proposed a National Federation of Dairy Cooperatives to transplant the spirit of Anand. NDDB followed in September 1965, with Kurien as chairman, to replicate the pattern.

Gayatri Patel told the *Times of India* that the family of Ramanbhai Punjabhai Patel had kept the room as it was. Shah, at the diamond jubilee of the society, called the overnight stay the step that led to NDDB.

A longer dialogue in which Shastri tells Kurien that the soil, the weather, and the buffaloes were ordinary, and that the organisation must therefore be the remarkable thing, circulates in retellings of Kurien’s autobiography. It is not in Damodaran’s report or in the *Times of India* piece. It is listed in [UNVERIFIED.md](../UNVERIFIED.md).

**Sources.** [*The Indian Express*, 2 March 2024](https://indianexpress.com/article/long-reads/union-code-0001-how-a-village-seeded-indias-white-revolution-9191324/); [*Times of India*](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/vadodara/countrys-first-dairy-cooperative-society-completes-75-glorious-years/articleshow/96386586.cms); [Meenesh Shah, NDDB, 20 December 2022](https://www.nddb.coop/about/speech/Diamond_Jubilee_function_of_Ajarpura_DCS_20_December_2022)

### Employee, not servant

**Who told it.** Tushaar Shah, who was interpreting.

**When and where.** IRMA’s first convocation, on the Amul lawns, with tens of thousands of farmers. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi presided. Shah does not give the date. Sriram, applying in early 1982, remembers seeing news that she had addressed that first convocation, so it fell before or during that admission season. IRMA had been founded in 1979.

**The story.** Kurien said that for forty years he had worked as an employee of farmers. Shah translated “employee” as *sevak*, a servant. Kurien cut him off: no, he was not a *sevak*. Shah froze. Indira Gandhi, sitting nearby, supplied the word *karmachari* — employee. Kurien seized it: yes, employee, employee, not servant. Shah writes that the farmers laughed, and that he wanted the ground to swallow him.

He adds, in a later note on the same LinkedIn post, that the mistake did not end the job. He went on translating, including an impromptu speech at Gujarat Vidyapeeth while Morarji Desai, the chancellor and a stickler for literary Gujarati, weighed each word. When Shah had a draft a day or two ahead he was all right. Impromptu work frightened him. Another translator, Dr Chothani, was tried and wandered too far from the English, so Shah stayed in demand. In Gujarat villages, farmers who had seen him at Kurien’s side sometimes recognised “Kurien’s translator,” which made the fieldwork easier.

There is a real tension with another memory. Shyam Benegal says Kurien often called himself a servant of the farmers, and meant it. Shah’s story is about the word Kurien wanted farmers to hear in Gujarati on that lawn. Both are in this archive. They are not forced to agree.

**Sources.** [Tushaar Shah, Vrikshamandir](https://vrikshamandir.com/2026/09/17/remembering-kurien-the-man-who-changed-countless-lives/); [LinkedIn post, including the note on later translations](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tushaar-shah-02277278_verghesekurien-amul-cooperatives-activity-7506054247218761728-aPJZ); [Hindustan Times, Shyam Benegal, 9 September 2012](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india/how-a-farmers-servant-painted-the-nation-white/story-w5muyu4dpzbC3Huk5XYRbK.html)

### Sell a refrigerator to an Eskimo

**Who told it.** Shantha Sheela Nair, then a junior officer, sitting in the back row.

**When and where.** Chennai, about 1979. She had just been appointed special officer for the oilseeds mission. Kurien had come to meet Chief Minister M. G. Ramachandran. She had not met Kurien before. That morning she had also been transferred.

**The story.** The two men were polite and a little stiff. Kurien spoke at his ease about Operation Flood. MGR was quiet. He was not convinced by the oilseeds agenda, or by packaged edible oil. Kurien then said that marketing is when you can sell a refrigerator to an Eskimo. Nair writes that MGR was won over and shook his hand. The chief secretary introduced her as the officer who would carry the project. Kurien said he had not met the lady, but that they gave and they took away. The chief secretary said she would do both jobs. MGR nodded.

**Source.** [Shantha Sheela Nair, Vrikshamandir, 30 May 2020](https://vrikshamandir.com/2020/05/30/memories-of-dr-v-kurien/)

## Other unique episodes

### A few more days, and a pasteuriser

**Who told it.** Amul Dairy’s own life of Kurien, for the arrival and the decision to stay. *The Hindu Business Line* of 9 September 2012, for the pasteuriser and the sum of money. The writer of that profile says the details come from the record of Kurien’s life as he and his colleagues told it.

**When and where.** Anand, from 13 May 1949 through the end of that year.

**The story.** He came to serve a bond at a government creamery, and to leave as soon as he could. The machinery Patel had leased kept failing. Kurien mended it after hours. He told Patel that the real answer was a plate pasteuriser, around Rs 60,000. Patel said he would find the money if Kurien would set it up and run it. The relieving order came. Bombay was the plan. Patel asked for a little more time, to get the cooperative’s equipment together. Amul’s page says he agreed to a few more days, and that the days became the rest of his working life.

**Sources.** [Amul Dairy](https://www.amuldairy.com/dr_kurien.php); [*The Hindu Business Line*, 9 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/opinion/The-man-who-empowered-through-milk/article20499872.ece)

### Two rupees from each farmer

**Who told it.** Shyam Benegal, in several published interviews. The fullest recent one is Soutik Biswas, BBC, 18 May 2024. Benegal told the same origin to *The Hindu*, and to the *Hindustan Times* on 9 September 2012, the day Kurien died. A 2012 interview with Subhash K. Jha in *The Times of India* adds how they met.

**When and where.** The mid-1970s. The film is *Manthan* (*The Churning*), released in 1976, 134 minutes. Benegal had been making documentaries for Kurien on Operation Flood. He told *The Hindu* their titles as he remembered them: *Operation Flood 1* and *Operation Flood 2*. Documentaries, he told the BBC, reached people already converted. He wanted a feature. Kurien said there was no money, because he would not take it from the farmers as a gift on top of their milk. The budget Benegal recalls to the *Hindustan Times* was about Rs 10 lakh.

**The story.** Kurien’s way through was to make the farmers the producers. Collection centres would deduct two rupees from each of about half a million members in Gujarat. Benegal told *The Hindu* a version of the pitch: the milk was eight rupees, take six, and the two rupees would make the film. “The farmers readily agreed because we were telling their story,” he told the BBC.

Vijay Tendulkar wrote the script after weeks among the farmers. Benegal chose one draft. Girish Karnad played the outsider who comes to start a cooperative. In the *Times of India* interview Benegal says they chose Karnad to play Kurien. The BBC describes the film as fiction: a government veterinarian, a private dairy owner, a village head, and a local milkman. Smita Patil, Naseeruddin Shah, Amrish Puri, Kulbhushan Kharbanda, and Mohan Agashe were in the cast. Vanraj Bhatia wrote the music. The shoot was 45 days. Shah told the BBC he lived in a hut, learned to make cow-dung cakes, and milked a buffalo.

Kurien opened the film in Gujarat. Farmers arrived in truckloads. Benegal says more prints were struck, across gauges from 35mm down to 8mm, than for other Indian films of the time, because Kurien then sent 16mm prints into villages with a team — a veterinarian, a milk technician, and a fodder specialist — to argue for cooperatives. The film was shown at the United Nations. It won a National Award. A later *Hindustan Times* report of Benegal’s remarks says two: best Hindi feature, and best screenplay for Tendulkar.

Benegal met Kurien in the 1960s, while working at the Advertising and Sales Promotion Company under Sylvester da Cunha on Amul advertising. He told the *Times of India* that Kurien saw himself as a servant of the milk farmers, and that people came to Kurien rather than Kurien going to them for help.

**Sources.** [BBC, 18 May 2024](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-india-69014789); [*The Hindu*, “Milkmen turned producers”](https://www.thehindu.com/features/magazine/milkmen-turned-producers/article6180423.ece); [*Hindustan Times*, 9 September 2012](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india/how-a-farmers-servant-painted-the-nation-white/story-w5muyu4dpzbC3Huk5XYRbK.html); [*Times of India*, Benegal to Subhash K. Jha](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/bollywood/news/manthan-made-on-rs-2-donations/articleshow/16348081.cms)

### A hundred Kuriens, and one Tribhuvandas

**Who told it.** Nirmala Kurien, at Anand on 26 November 2018, the 97th anniversary of her father’s birth.

**The story.** She said his greatest worry was that IRMA might produce a hundred more Kuriens, and that a Tribhuvandas would still be missing. She called Patel the rarest kind of politician: honest, patriotic, fair, and noble. Her father, she said, never doubted that farmers could work harder and produce more. He had chosen to live in Anand for almost 64 years, and he had worked, lived, and died in Gujarat.

**Source.** [Indian Express, 27 November 2018](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/father-atheist-worked-entire-life-farmers-employee-dr-verghese-kurien-amul-5466268/)
