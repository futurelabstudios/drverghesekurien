# 2. Anand, Amul & the Kaira Union (1946–1973)

## The problem: Polson and the Bombay Milk Scheme

In the 1940s, milk from Kaira (Kheda) district was collected by traders and the private dairy **Polson**, which held an effective monopoly on supplying the **Bombay Milk Scheme**. Farmers had little say in the price and were often paid poorly and late.

## The farmers organise (1946)

- Local leader **Tribhuvandas Kishibhai Patel** brought farmers together. **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** advised them to form their own cooperative and supply Bombay directly, and sent **Morarji Desai** to help organise them.
- At a meeting in **Chaklasi**, farmers resolved to stop supplying Polson.
- The **Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers' Union Ltd.** was registered on **14 December 1946**.
- Tribhuvandas Patel served as its chairman until he retired in the 1970s.

## Kurien arrives (1949)

Kurien was posted to the government's research creamery at Anand in 1949, unhappy and keen to leave. He began helping the cooperative with its broken equipment in the evenings, and Tribhuvandas Patel convinced him to stay. From 1950 he was the Union's General Manager. The partnership of the farmer-leader and the engineer-manager became the template for everything that followed: **farmers own and govern; professionals manage**.

## The buffalo-milk breakthrough

Most of Kaira's milk came from buffaloes, and the established view held that buffalo milk could not be made into skim milk powder or infant food. **H. M. Dalaya**, Kurien's fellow dairy engineering student in the US, came to Anand and developed the process. This let a farmer cooperative compete with **Nestlé** and **Glaxo** in condensed milk and baby food, and handle the "flush season" when milk was plentiful.

## Growth

- Procurement grew from about **200 litres a day (1948) to 20,000 litres a day (1952)**.
- **1955** — products sold under the brand **Amul** (often linked to the Sanskrit *amulya*, "priceless"; ⚠️ help us find a primary source).
- **1956** — after Nestlé refused to share technology, Kurien expanded condensed milk production; imports were banned two years later.
- **1964** — Amul cattle feed factory inaugurated by PM Lal Bahadur Shastri.
- Later work with **G. H. Wilster** made cheese from buffalo milk possible.
- **1966** — the **Amul girl** was created by Sylvester daCunha's agency (the "Thoroughbred" butter ad). Kurien protected the topical, often satirical campaign for decades.
- **1973** — Amul's 25th anniversary year; **GCMMF** formed (see [Chapter 4](04-gcmmf.md)).

## The "Anand pattern"

The three-tier structure that Operation Flood later copied across India:

1. **Village Dairy Cooperative Society** — every farmer who pours milk is a member; paid by fat and quantity, twice daily.
2. **District Milk Union** — owns the dairy plants, chilling centres, cattle feed and veterinary services.
3. **State Federation** — marketing under a common brand.

Profits flow back to the producers. Professionals work *for* the farmers' elected boards.

## Today

Amul's member base is about **3.6 million** milk producers, and its group turnover was about **₹90,000 crore** in FY25 (per Wikipedia, citing company figures).
