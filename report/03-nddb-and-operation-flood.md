# 3. NDDB & Operation Flood


## Why NDDB was created

<figure class="fig narrow float-right"><img src="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri_%28from_stamp%29.jpg?width=600" alt="Lal Bahadur Shastri" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri, who visited Anand in October 1964 and asked Kurien to take the Anand pattern across India. India Post stamp, 1966. <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lal_Bahadur_Shastri_(from_stamp).jpg">India Post, GODL-India</a></figcaption></figure>


In **October 1964**, Prime Minister **Lal Bahadur Shastri** came to Anand to open Amul's cattle feed factory. Impressed, he asked Kurien to replicate the Anand pattern nationwide. The result was the **National Dairy Development Board (NDDB)**, founded on **16 July 1965** as a society based in Anand, with Kurien as chairman. Kurien insisted it be located in Anand, close to farmers, not in Delhi.

## Key milestones

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1965 | NDDB founded at Anand (16 July) |
| 1970 | Operation Flood launched; the **Indian Dairy Corporation (IDC)** set up to receive and manage food aid |
| 1979 | Oilseeds Growers' Cooperative Project begins |
| 1979 | IRMA founded (initially linked to Operation Flood's second phase) |
| 1987 | **NDDB Act, 1987** (Act 37 of 1987) takes effect on **12 October 1987**; NDDB merges with IDC and becomes an institution of national importance |
| 1998 | India becomes the world's largest milk producer; Kurien steps down; Dr. Amrita Patel becomes chair (1998–2014) |
| 2021 | Dr. Meenesh Shah appointed chairman |

## Operation Flood

Described by NDDB as **"the world's largest food-aid-for-development program."** The idea: sell donated milk powder and butter oil (from the European Economic Community, via the World Food Programme) inside India, and use the proceeds to build farmer-owned cooperatives. Support also came from FAO, UNICEF and the World Bank.

| Phase | Years* | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| I | 1970–1979/81 | 18 milksheds linked to the four metro markets (Delhi, Bombay, Calcutta, Madras) |
| II | 1979/81–1985 | Expansion to more than 290 towns. By 1985: about **43,000 village cooperatives** and **4.25 million** producers |
| III | 1985–1996 | Making cooperatives self-sustaining; infrastructure; training professionals (including at IRMA) |

\* ⚠️ Sources give slightly different phase dates. See [Open questions](12-open-questions.md).

**Outcomes**

- Between 1970 and 1998, India's milk output roughly **quadrupled** with NDDB's support.
- By 1998, about **81,000 dairy cooperatives** had been formed with NDDB's help.
- In **1998**, India overtook the United States as the world's largest milk producer. By 2010–11 it produced about **17%** of world output.
- The "billion-litre idea" — Kurien's ambition to turn dispersed small producers into an organised national supply.

## NDDB subsidiaries today

Mother Dairy; Indian Dairy Machinery Company (IDMC); Indian Immunologicals Ltd. (IIL), Hyderabad; NDDB Dairy Services; NDDB Mrida; NDDB CALF; Mother Dairy Fruit & Vegetable Pvt. Ltd.
