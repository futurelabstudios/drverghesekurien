# Life

Verghese Kurien was born on 26 November 1921 in Kozhikode (then Calicut), in the Malabar district of the Madras Presidency, now Kerala. Amul’s own account names his father as Puthen Parakkal Kurien, a civil surgeon. His daughter, Nirmala Kurien, said in 2018 that he lost his father when he was very young.

He is survived in the public record by his wife, Molly Kurien, their daughter Nirmala, and a grandson, Siddharth. Molly died a few months after him. Nirmala said her mother, too, was cremated. R. S. Sodhi, then managing director of the Gujarat Cooperative Milk Marketing Federation, recalled that Kurien was cremated at the same place as Tribhuvandas Patel, at the family’s wish.

## Education

Amul and the Gujarat federation’s 2012 note agree on the schooling path they choose to tell: a science degree from Loyola College in 1940, an engineering degree from the College of Engineering, Guindy, in Chennai, then a graduate apprenticeship at the Tata Iron and Steel Company in Jamshedpur. He left TISCO for a Government of India scholarship in dairy engineering, trained at the Imperial Institute of Animal Husbandry and Dairying in Bangalore, and completed a master’s degree at Michigan State University in 1948. Amul describes that degree as mechanical engineering with dairy engineering as a minor.

The World Food Prize biography gives the same universities and the same year at Michigan State, and calls the American degree a master of science in mechanical engineering with distinction. It also says he did special studies at the Tata technical institute in 1946 and nine months of dairy training in Bangalore. The order of those years is smoother in Amul’s account: Guindy, then TISCO, then the scholarship.

A profile in *The Hindu Business Line* on 9 September 2012, written by someone who had interviewed him, adds that he left TISCO because the company was headed by a maternal grand-uncle, John Mathai, later India’s finance minister, and that being known as the boss’s relation was stifling. The scholarship was for dairy engineering. Metallurgy had been his preference. On return he had an offer from Union Carbide in Kolkata that the government bond would not let him take.

In interviews published by Rasheeda Bhagat, he told a different campus story: that he had been sent to study dairy engineering in Pittsburgh, and that he “cheated” the government by studying metallurgy and nuclear physics because he wanted to be a nuclear engineer. The institutions that employed him, and the World Food Prize, place the degree at Michigan State and describe it as mechanical engineering. This archive does not choose a private memory over that public record. Both are reported in [Stories](stories/anecdotes.md).

Michigan State’s own AgBioResearch article of 10 January 2018 puts his arrival on campus in the winter of 1946, and says he came with little interest in dairy, on a scholarship he had won by answering that pasteurisation was something to do with milk. At East Lansing he argued with fellow students over subjects that interested him and won a tennis championship. The article agrees on the year, 1948, and on mechanical engineering, but gives the minor as nuclear physics, not dairy engineering as Amul has it. That puts nuclear physics on the Michigan State record, as Kurien told Bhagat, though at Michigan rather than Pittsburgh, and as a minor rather than the whole degree. The archive keeps Amul’s minor and the university’s side by side. *The Hindu* of 8 July 2016, quoting D. V. Mallikarjuna, an engineer with the Shivamogga cooperative milk union, also gives a master’s in mechanical engineering from Michigan State in 1948.

His daughter Nirmala, speaking at a Michigan State memorial talk in the autumn of 2017, said he had been taken aback in the United States by racial slurs and by segregated buses. Her account is in [Stories](stories/anecdotes.md). The university kept the tie after his death, with a bust in 2016 and that memorial talk. Both are in [Awards](awards.md).

## The man who arrived in Anand

He reached Anand on Friday, 13 May 1949, posted to the government creamery to serve his bond. Amul says his aim was to get free of that bond and leave. At the end of 1949 he had a relieving order and was ready for Bombay. Tribhuvandas Kishibhai Patel, chairman of the Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers’ Union, asked him to stay and help assemble the cooperative’s dairy equipment. He stayed.

The *Business Line* profile of 9 September 2012 fills in the scene from interviews. Kurien was 27. He had thought of Anand as “somewhere near Bombay,” and found it was not. The writer gives his pay as Rs 275 a month. Bhagat, recalling her own interviews, gives Rs 350 and the rank of a Class I gazetted officer. The two figures are left as the two journalists recorded them. The same profile says he disliked milk, that it was never a favourite food, that he was a self-proclaimed atheist, a meat-eater, and not particularly averse to alcohol, and that he understood Gujarati and chose not to speak it so that people would talk freely in front of him.

Nirmala Kurien said in November 2018 that religion played no part in his life, that he was an atheist, and that he was cremated according to his wishes. She was answering a public allegation, discussed in [Debates](debates.md). She also said he worked his whole life as an employee of farmers, and that he loved Tribhuvandas Patel as a father. In her words, Dalaya was a brother to him. Without either man, she said, Amul would not have happened.

He and Molly lived simply enough that visitors remarked on it. Ajay Gupta, an alumnus of the Institute of Rural Management Anand, described a short visit to the Anand house in June 2012: ordinary furniture, tea made and served by Molly. That visit is told in full in the [anecdotes](stories/anecdotes.md).

## Death

Kurien died at about 1.15 a.m. on 9 September 2012 at Muljibhai Patel Urological Hospital in Nadiad, near Anand, after a brief illness. Sodhi told NDTV it was a natural death, and that Kurien had had kidney trouble. He was 90. Amul’s press note that morning said 91. Counting from 26 November 1921, his 91st birthday had not yet come.

The body was taken to his residence and then placed in Sardar Hall at the Amul dairy in Anand so that people could pay respects. The federation said the cremation was set for 4.30 that afternoon.

Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, President Pranab Mukherjee, and Vice President Hamid Ansari sent condolences. Ansari’s message, as NDTV reported it, said Kurien had engineered the White Revolution and was the man behind Amul.
