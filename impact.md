# Impact

The numbers below are the ones printed by Amul, the World Food Prize, the Food and Agriculture Organization, or a named journalist. They are not adjusted to a common year. A figure from 1998 and a figure from 2012 answer different questions.

## Milk

The World Food Prize, recording the position at Kurien’s retirement from NDDB in 1998, says Operation Flood then reached more than 10 million producers in 81,000 cooperatives, sending almost five million tonnes of milk a year to more than 1,000 towns. The same page’s summary speaks of 86,000 cooperatives and nearly 250 million people whose nutrition was improved by dairy foods. In 1970, it says, procurement inside the programme was 190,000 tonnes, from 278,000 farmers.

India became the world’s largest milk producer in 1998, ahead of the United States. That is the prize foundation’s statement, repeated in its tribute when he died.

Amul’s 2012 note, looking past his retirement, says Indian milk production moved from 20 million metric tonnes a year in the 1960s to 122 million in 2011. In Gujarat, the federation then had 16,100 village societies and 3.2 million members pouring milk twice a day. NDDB’s chairman, Meenesh Shah, said in December 2022 that India contributed about a fifth of the world’s milk. The BBC, in May 2024, said nearly a quarter. The share moved. Both sentences are left as said.

Amul’s own institutional page describes an Operation Flood investment of about Rs 1,700 crore over 25 years, and a milk economy it then valued at about Rs 55,000 crore a year. It calls the programme India’s largest rural employment scheme. Citing the National Council of Applied Economic Research, it says that in Operation Flood areas more than 75 per cent of rural households with milch animals were small or marginal farmers, or landless, and that nearly 70 per cent of village-society members came from households with one or two animals.

NDDB’s corporate film says that by the end of Operation Flood the grid covered more than 72,000 village societies in more than 300 towns. That film is linked in [Videos](media/videos.md). Its number is the board’s later telling, not the 1998 prize text.

Sources: [World Food Prize](https://www.worldfoodprize.org/blog/people/dr-verghese-kurien/); [Amul, 9 September 2012](https://www.amul.com/index.php?q=m/dr-v-kurien); [Amul Dairy](https://www.amuldairy.com/dr_kurien.php); [NDDB, 20 December 2022](https://www.nddb.coop/about/speech/Diamond_Jubilee_function_of_Ajarpura_DCS_20_December_2022); [BBC, 18 May 2024](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-india-69014789).

## The farmer’s rupee

The design aim was that farmers would own processing and marketing, not only the animal. A *Business Line* profile on 9 September 2012 said the Gujarat unions were then paying an average of Rs 470 per kilogram of milk fat. For full-cream milk at 6 per cent fat, the writer calculated about Rs 29 a litre, or about three-quarters of the consumer price in Ahmedabad or Delhi. That ratio is the writer’s arithmetic for 2012, offered as evidence of Kurien’s rule that the cream of the business is in the brand, and that the brand should belong to the producers.

In March 2024 Harish Damodaran reported that Ajarpura’s society paid an average of Rs 43.01 a litre in 2022–23, including a year-end price difference of Rs 7.46. The same article says Ajarpura is no longer a star society. Patidar families, who once led the cooperatives, have in large numbers left dairying and left India. The pourers and the chairpersons in many Gujarat unions are now from other communities. Damodaran’s judgment is that neither Kurien nor Tribhuvandas Patel would have objected. Milk, for them, was a tool for the rural producer, whoever that producer had become.

Gayatriben Riteshbhai Patel, chairperson of Ajarpura since June 2022, told him the business can pay, and that it has little social status. Parents, she said, do not want to marry a daughter to someone whose work is cattle. She wants farming and dairying in the school curriculum. That is a present-tense limit on the revolution, reported from the village where Shastri slept.

Source: [*The Hindu Business Line*, 9 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/opinion/The-man-who-empowered-through-milk/article20499872.ece); [*The Indian Express*, 2 March 2024](https://indianexpress.com/article/long-reads/union-code-0001-how-a-village-seeded-indias-white-revolution-9191324/).

## Institutions that outlived the chairman

He left behind organisations with their own legal lives: the Kaira union, GCMMF and the Amul brand, NDDB, IRMA, Anandalaya, Vidya Dairy, the oilseeds effort and the Dhara brand, and a national dairy federation he helped reshape in 1988. IRMA’s page on its founder says the point of the school was a generation of managers for an economy that professional management had ignored.

Other countries asked for the pattern. The World Food Prize says the cooperative movement became a model for developing countries and for international agencies. NDTV’s obituary reported that the World Bank had treated the Amul model as a tool against poverty, and that GCMMF was receiving requests from African countries to help them copy it. A detailed Pakistan mission in 1982 is mentioned in secondary bibliographies and was not opened here. It is in [UNVERIFIED.md](UNVERIFIED.md).

The argument about whether NDDB, after him, kept faith with farmer-owned marketing is in [Debates](debates.md).

## Women, caste, and the queue

Amul’s 2012 note says a majority of the cooperative members in the Gujarat system were women. Kurien’s own speeches, as alumni remember them, treated the twice-daily queue as a social fact as well as a logistics fact: a Brahmin standing behind a Dalit because the Dalit came first. M. S. Sriram, who heard that speech and was moved by it, later wrote that the same first-come rule applies to cinema tickets, and that the queue did not by itself break caste. Both the claim and the alumnus’s doubt are part of the impact, and both are sourced in [Stories](stories/anecdotes.md).

## What “White Revolution” is doing in the name

The phrase, as Amul and the World Food Prize use it, names the rise in Indian milk after Operation Flood, set beside the Green Revolution in grain. It is Kurien’s public title — Father of the White Revolution — more than it is a statute. The operational name of the programme is Operation Flood.
