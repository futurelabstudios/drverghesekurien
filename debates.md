# Debates

This page is short on purpose. It records arguments that have a public source, and it names who made them. It does not try to close them.

## Commodity aid: gift, or dependence?

Operation Flood was financed in large part by selling donated European skim milk powder and butter oil, and using the money to build Indian cooperatives. The Food and Agriculture Organization’s account, which follows NDDB’s own explanation, says Kurien saw the European surplus as both a threat and a chance. Dumped cheap dairy products could have finished a weak Indian industry. Used as an investment, the same powder and butter oil could build a market that Indian farmers would later fill. Phase I, from 1970, rested on a World Food Programme agreement for 126,000 tonnes of skim milk powder and 42,000 tonnes of butter oil.

Critics said the opposite: that a poor country would become hooked on Europe’s surplus, and that aid would push down the price farmers received. The abstract of a Durham University thesis on dairy aid and Operation Flood records those charges, including the fear of permanent dependence, and also records the counter from the programme’s officials: they were repairing India’s dairy marketing system. The abstract’s own reading of the data is that Indian production and consumption rose, that India moved toward self-sufficiency, and that pricing mistakes which at first hurt farmers were corrected. The thesis was not read beyond the abstract for this archive. The Durham page opened here did not print the author’s name in the abstract view this research used, so the name is not added from memory.

Shanti George’s book *Operation Flood: An Appraisal of Current Indian Dairy Policy* (1985) is the best-known book-length critique. It was not opened. It is named in [UNVERIFIED.md](UNVERIFIED.md) so it is not silently omitted and not silently summarised.

Source: [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/t3080t/t3080T07.htm); [Durham e-thesis abstract](http://etheses.dur.ac.uk/4682/).

## Who owns the cooperative once it is large?

M. S. Sriram, an IRMA alumnus, put the contradiction in a 2006 essay. Kurien argued that farmers should hold their own resources. He also spent decades in the chair of bodies whose members were farmers, while he himself was the professional from Kerala. Sriram’s question is whether that chair, on Kurien’s own principles, belonged to a farmer. Sriram’s answer is uncomfortable on purpose: Kurien had no private stake in the milk, which made him a useful balance against vested interests, and he still occupied a seat the cooperative idea assigned to a producer.

Amrita Patel, who followed him at NDDB, told Rediff in 2012 that his message was the one many Indians still miss. A cooperative, in his telling, is not a government department. It is a business. The members are the shareholders. It has to pay for itself. He developed markets first, she said, and let the market pull production, rather than starting with inputs. To the end, she said, he argued that the cooperatives had to hire professionals to market the milk.

Source: [Sriram, 17 April 2006](http://joy-of-books.blogspot.com/2006/04/kurien-personal-essay.html); [Rediff, 12 September 2012](http://www.rediff.com/money/report/interview-amrita-patel-on-kurien-ideologies/20120912.htm).

## The break with NDDB, and the resignation of 2006

In a *Frontline* interview published on 28 March 2003, Kurien, then 82, was strongly critical of Amrita Patel, his successor at NDDB, over plans for joint ventures with state cooperatives. The interview’s headline quoted him saying NDDB had no marketing capability. This research used the interview’s framing, not a full transcript.

On 20 March 2006 he resigned as chairman of GCMMF. *Frontline* (Anupama Katakam, 7 April 2006) reported that the board had intended a no-confidence vote on 24 March, and that he stepped down ahead of it, after 33 years. In the federation’s release, as *Frontline* quotes it, he said the move pained him, and that he was also deferring to a Gujarat High Court order: co-opted members could not continue on cooperative management committees. He said he believed in the rule of law and in cooperative principle. *The Economic Times* reported the speech in a crowded IRMA auditorium.

Sriram’s essay, written while this was fresh, describes a seminar on those joint ventures and a press briefing he felt misrepresented the room. Alumni who commented on the essay split. Some felt the same betrayal. Others wrote that Kurien was right to resist a structure in which a government body, through NDDB companies, would hold the majority, and that the joint ventures later failed. This archive does not audit those ventures. It records the split among people who were in the room.

A *Business Line* column on 9 September 2012 argued that NDDB had since turned Mother Dairy toward private suppliers and imported powder, and that farmer cooperatives had weakened outside Gujarat and Karnataka. That is the columnist’s charge in an obituary week. It is not a finding of this archive.

Kurien’s own bureaucratic fear, as Rasheeda Bhagat reports it from her interviews, was that the IAS would take NDDB the day he died. He told her he had refused to release NDDB funds to the Kerala federation because an IAS officer headed it. She also reports a former commerce secretary, whom she does not name, claiming that even a government-to-government request from King Birendra of Nepal for ghee had to pass through “the ghee czar.” The secretary is unnamed, so the Nepal episode is in [UNVERIFIED.md](UNVERIFIED.md). The IAS quarrel is his, told to a named journalist.

Sources: [*Frontline*, 28 March 2003](https://frontline.thehindu.com/the-nation/article30216152.ece); [*Frontline*, 7 April 2006](https://frontline.thehindu.com/the-nation/milkmans-exit/article30208954.ece); [*The Economic Times*, 21 March 2006](https://m.economictimes.com/indias-milkman-kurien-quits-amul/articleshow/1457434.cms); [Rasheeda Bhagat, 13 September 2012](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/doodh-ka-doodh-kurien-style/article23032255.ece).

## An allegation about conversions

In November 2018, Dileep Sanghani, then vice-chairman of NAFED and a former Gujarat minister, said at a reception in Amreli that Kurien had given farmers’ money to Christian missionaries for conversions in the Dangs. Nirmala Kurien, at Anand on 26 November 2018, said her father was an atheist, that religion played no part in his life, and that he was cremated according to his wishes, as was her mother. She said he worked as an employee of the farmers. She did not think it worth naming Sanghani. R. S. Sodhi, managing director of GCMMF, said Kurien’s religion was the religion of each farmer, and recalled that the family had asked for cremation, at the place where Tribhuvandas Patel was cremated.

The charge and the denial are both public. The denial is from his daughter and from the federation’s managing director. This archive found no document, in the pages it opened, of Amul funds used for conversions.

Sources: [Indian Express, 27 November 2018](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/he-was-an-atheist-religion-played-no-part-in-his-life-says-kuriens-daughter-5466180/); [Indian Express, Sodhi and Nirmala](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/ignore-such-remarks-verghese-kuriens-daughter-on-conversion-charge-5464927/).
