## Anecdotes from people who knew him
{: .no_toc }

<p class="lede">Stories told by colleagues, students and witnesses — each linked to where it was first told. Have one of your own? <a href="{{ '/memories/' | relative_url }}">Add it to the archive.</a></p>

<div class="anecdotes">
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>On the IIM Ahmedabad board, Kurien argued that graduates should serve India before heading abroad. An industrialist mocked the idea with a jibe about cigars. Kurien's reply: "No, you continue to teach them how to suck on cigars." He never went back, and built IRMA instead.</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">IR</span><span class="who"><strong>IRMA's own retelling</strong><span>IRMA alumnus and communications manager, 2019</span><a href="https://thelogicalindian.com/irma-anand-institute/">The Logical Indian, 2019 ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>When Morarji Desai asked why so much care was going into IRMA's campus, Kurien said the institute meant to produce kings — and kings cannot be produced in a pig-sty. By kings, he meant people who would think and dream big.</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">TS</span><span class="who"><strong>Tushaar Shah</strong><span>Former IRMA director; Kurien's Gujarati translator from 1980</span><a href="https://www.irma.ac.in/programmes/convocation/speech/71">IRMA Convocation address, 2017 ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>Kurien rehearsed his speeches with his young translator and asked him to match his tone and his pauses in Gujarati. At a dairy inauguration in 1984 he insisted farmers be called karmachaari — those who work and employ — never sevak, servants.</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">TS</span><span class="who"><strong>Tushaar Shah</strong><span>On translating for him at farmer meetings</span><a href="https://www.irma.ac.in/programmes/convocation/speech/71">IRMA Convocation address, 2017 ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>IRMA's approval sat in the agriculture ministry for over two years. So Kurien invited Prime Minister Indira Gandhi to be chief guest at the first convocation. She accepted — and the file was cleared almost at once.</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">KS</span><span class="who"><strong>Katar Singh</strong><span>Worked with Kurien for 22 years; IRMA director until 2002</span><a href="https://www.irma.ac.in/faculty-research/research-and-publications/network/current-and-past-issues?issueid=178&tab=2">IRMA Network tribute ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>He once asked the faculty how many of them had actually managed an organisation. His point: you cannot really teach management if you have never done it. "Rural", he insisted, never means "backward".</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">KS</span><span class="who"><strong>Katar Singh</strong><span>On Kurien and the IRMA faculty</span><a href="https://www.irma.ac.in/faculty-research/research-and-publications/network/current-and-past-issues?issueid=178&tab=2">IRMA Network tribute ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure class="anecdote">
    <blockquote>When the young engineer arrived in Anand, nobody would rent him a room — he was a Christian, a meat-eater and a bachelor. He lived in a garage for a long time, in the town he would never leave.</blockquote>
    <figcaption><span class="avatar">BT</span><span class="who"><strong>As recalled in Business Today</strong><span>On his first years in Anand, 1949</span><a href="https://archives.digitaltoday.in/businesstoday/20001121/cover3.html">Business Today, 2000 ↗</a></span></figcaption>
  </figure>
</div>
